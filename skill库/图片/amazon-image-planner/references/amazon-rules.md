# Amazon Image Rules

Use these rules as compliance and planning guidance. They summarize Amazon image compliance, listing-gallery planning, and A+ content image guidance.

## Listing Baseline

- Every product needs a compliant `MAIN` image. A strong listing gallery usually adds six secondary images.
- Technical baseline: sharp, clear, non-pixelated still images. Common accepted formats include JPEG/JPG, PNG, TIFF, and non-animated GIF. Longest side should generally be 500-10,000 px.
- Every image must accurately represent the sold product and match the product title.
- Keep colors, quantity, scale, variants, and included accessories truthful.
- Do not show confusing props or accessories as if they are included.

## MAIN Image

Plan `MAIN` as a clean product identification image:

- Pure white background, RGB 255,255,255.
- Product fills about 85% of the frame.
- Show the complete product once, uncropped.
- Preserve true color, proportion, quantity, and included accessories.
- No text, logo, watermark, border, color block, badge, graphic overlay, price, review quote, coupon, or decorative layout.
- No Amazon, Prime, Alexa, Amazon Choice, Premium Choice, Best Seller, hot-sale badge, marketplace mark, or lookalike mark.
- Do not show packaging unless the packaging is an actual product feature or sold bundle evidence.

## Secondary Listing Images

Use secondary slots for product understanding:

- Lifestyle/product scene: realistic use environment, product as hero, no misleading extra included items.
- Detail: materials, finish, structure, texture, ports, seams, craftsmanship, surface evidence.
- Scale/dimensions: believable size context or a dedicated size chart.
- Contents/bundle: every included item at fair visual importance.
- Steps/use: installation, assembly, operation, or care process.
- Benefit/info image: concise US-English copy, callouts, icons, and product evidence when useful.

Include 1-2 text-free lifestyle/product-scene images when the gallery benefits from real context. Text-free scene images must not contain headlines, captions, callouts, labels, arrows, icons, badges, benefit copy, feature copy, or marketing slogans.

## A+ Baseline

- A+ content should be unique to the product and brand story, not just a duplicate of listing gallery images.
- Use RGB JPG/PNG/BMP assets, at least 72 dpi, sharp and non-blurry, under 2 MB per final upload.
- Keep key content in a central safe area.
- Use mobile-readable copy, clear hierarchy, balanced spacing, coherent lighting, product continuity, and consistent design logic.
- Do not use tiny text, watermarks, animation, QR codes, contact details, external URLs, or hyperlinks.
- Use real brand names as text only when supplied. Do not invent logos, trademarks, brand history, authorization claims, websites, or contact details.
- A+ modules may have external textTitle/textBody outside the image; those do not need to be drawn into the image.

## Prohibited Or Risky Content

Avoid:

- Prices, discounts, coupons, free shipping, sales urgency, and purchase calls to action such as buy now or add to cart.
- Customer reviews, star ratings, seller-specific claims, seller authorization claims, comparison against named competitors.
- Unsupported guarantees, satisfaction claims, awards, certifications, eco claims, medical/cure/prevention claims, or exaggerated claims such as best, top-rated, bestseller.
- Time-sensitive claims such as new, latest, sale, now, currently, or holiday urgency unless the user explicitly provides defensible context.
- Amazon page UI, Amazon/Prime/Alexa marks, marketplace badges, and lookalike marks.
- Nudity, sexually suggestive content, or content that conflicts with product category policies.

## Special Guards

Size chart only:

- If a slot is a size chart, dimension chart, size options image, measurement infographic, spec diagram, or dimensional comparison image, make that slot dedicated to size/spec information only.
- Do not mix usage scenarios, lifestyle icons, benefit claims, emotional copy, or marketing slogans into a size chart.

Small benefit icons:

- Each small icon or badge in the same image must communicate a different benefit exactly once.
- Do not repeat the same selling point across icon rows, callouts, headlines, or panels.
- Prefer fewer unique icons over a crowded row of repeated benefits.
